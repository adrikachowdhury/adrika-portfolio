Undergraduate Group Thesis · 2025

Exploring the Climate Change Dynamics through Multi-Aspect Classification

Tustee Mazumdar and Adrika Chowdhury

Supervisor: Dr. Abu Nowshed Chy; Co-supervisor: Dr. Tarem Ahmed

Department of Computer Science, Asian University for Women.

This research explores climate change discourse on social media through multiple text classification tasks. Using a collection of climate-related tweets, we investigated different aspects of public conversations around climate change.

Dataset: ClimaConvo

We compiled ClimaConvo, a dataset of 15,309 English climate-related tweets collected through the official X/Twitter API between 1 January and 30 December 2022. The dataset was used to investigate six classification tasks.

Classification Tasks

  • Relevance: Identifying whether a tweet is relevant to climate change.
  • Stance: Identifying the position expressed towards climate change.
  • Hate Speech: Detecting hateful content in climate-related discussions.
  • Direction: Classifying the direction of the expressed climate discourse.
  • Targets: Identifying the target of the discussion.
  • Humour: Identifying humorous content in climate-related tweets.

Model Development

The study compared four Transformer-based models: BERT, RoBERTa, DistilBERT, and ClimateBERT. Their representations were further processed through BiLSTM and MLP layers for classification.

The data was divided into training and testing sets using an 80:20 split. Preprocessing included text normalisation, emoji and emoticon handling, stopword removal, and stemming. SMOTE was used to address class imbalance during training.

Evaluation and Findings

Models were evaluated using macro-averaged F1-score to account for class imbalance. DistilBERT achieved the strongest overall performance, with an average macro-F1 score of 77.8% across the six classification tasks.

The reported average represents performance across the task-specific evaluations, rather than the score of a single classification task.

Published Conference Paper · 2025

ClimateNet: A Multi-Faceted Classifier for Analyzing Climate Change Discourse

Mazumdar, T.*, Chowdhury, A.*, Chy, A. N., & Ahmed, T.

2025 IEEE 13th Region 10 Humanitarian Technology Conference (R10-HTC), pp. 105-110.

This paper presents ClimateNet, a multi-faceted classification approach for analysing climate change discourse on social media. It examines different dimensions of climate-related conversations to provide a more comprehensive understanding of public discourse.
